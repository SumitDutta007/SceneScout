The ordering logic, which the tool encodes in `rank.posting_order`: post the premise first (jilted bride → stranger), from the earliest episodes, so a new viewer who taps through lands on ep 1–5 and binges forward. Rotate hook types (humiliation → romance/proposal → violence → reveal) so consecutive posts don't feel the same. Hold late-season payoffs.

## 3. Quality check: where the tool picks badly

### Bad picks in the top 15

| Pick | Problem | Root cause |
|---|---|---|
| **#1 (Ep 4 + 5)** | It ends on "Come." as the couple walk off in the rain. The story comes to rest, so it isn't a cliffhanger. The tool ranked it first. | The script notes a "melancholic orchestral swell…fades to black". The rules reward the **written music note**, not whether the question is still open. |
| **#3 (Ep 29 + 38)** | It ends on a "warm, defiant smile". The outrage hook is good, but it lands on comfort, and the two pieces are 9 episodes apart. | Same sting-word bias. The stitch scorer doesn't model emotional direction across the join. |
| **#13 (Ep 16)** | The best beat (the thug's confession "Gerald Harrington paid us!") is in the middle, and the clip ends on a tender look. | Each window is scored only at its two ends. Nothing scores the pacing or the peak *inside* the window. |
| **#15 (Ep 18)** | The fire is only the first 2s, then 50s of embrace and grief. The real promo is the rescue from the inferno earlier in the episode. | Same: an opening shock plus an ending question beats a clip that is tense throughout. |

### Systematic weaknesses (what to expect on new shows)

1. **Keywords stand in for meaning.** Hook triggers include stage-direction adjectives ("sneering grin", "glare", a judge's gavel "striking" the desk) and props. Reveals carried by what a line *means* are invisible. These are exactly the hooks microdrama relies on, which is why the Claude judge exists.
2. **It depends on how the writer marks music.** Cliffhanger detection leans on notes like "swell", "sting", "cliffhanger". A show whose scripts don't describe music will score much worse, and a show that marks every scene ending as "dramatic" will look full of cliffhangers.
3. **Only the ends of a clip are scored.** A sagging middle (#15) or a peak buried mid-clip (#13) isn't penalised.
4. **Stitch bias.** A stitched promo picks its hook piece and its ending piece independently, so it outscores continuous clips. Before I capped stitches at ⅓ of the list they took 10 of 15 slots, and the cap then pushed out the best ep 1 stitch. The stitcher also once joined two unrelated plot threads (a garden fight with the villains' manor scene). Requiring an on-screen shared character fixed that one case. Continuity is only flagged, not checked.
5. **Diversity rules can drop the best version.** The ep 1 stitch (0.71) lost its slot to the overlapping ep 1 single (0.68) because the stitch cap was full. The tool keeps only one version of a moment, and its tie-breaking isn't editorial.
6. **Hook-type labels are crude.** Ep 36 is labelled "humiliation" because it contains "marrying". That makes the posting-order rotation noisy.
7. **Script time ≠ final cut.** All timestamps are planned script times. The delivered episode may drift by a second or two, so boundaries should be re-snapped on the actual video (shot detection or a transcript alignment) before cutting.
8. **Coverage.** 31 of 50 episodes contribute no promo. That's expected, since promos cluster on big beats, but the mid-season poisoning arc (ep 19–27) is under-represented because its hooks are reveals, not violence.

### What I'd do next

- **Run hybrid mode.** `--mode hybrid` with a key adds the Claude judge, which reads each full episode and is told to refine or reject the rule candidates. Misses #1–#3 above are exactly what it should fix. Its scores are blended with the rules, and large disagreements are flagged for a human. It hasn't been measured yet.
- **Build a gold set.** Have the editor log the promos they actually cut for 5–10 back-catalogue shows, then run `--gold picks.json` to track precision@k and temporal IoU per tool change. After that, track the editor's accept rate on each week's new shows, and finally the promos' real performance (3-second hold rate, CTR to ep 1). That last signal is the only one that really settles "best promo".
- **Cheap rule fixes:** score the peak inside the window, not just its ends; down-weight stage-direction adjectives; learn the cliffhanger threshold from the gold set; extend the unknown-name check to every named speaker.

## 4. Hybrid run (Gemini 3 Flash): what the LLM changed

`python -m promo_finder "data/..." --mode hybrid --provider gemini --model gemini-3-flash-preview`. **12 calls (5 episodes per call), 2.5 min, 269k input + 49k output tokens, $0 on the free tier.** The validator dropped 5 of 56 LLM proposals (lengths up to 73.7s, or out-of-order stitches). Log: `raw_outputs/hybrid_gemini/run_log.txt`.

**Against the 12 moments from §2–3** (`eval/my_picks_gold.json`, scores in `raw_outputs/eval_vs_my_picks.json`; a moment counts as found if a promo covers ≥50% of it):

| | Found anywhere in the candidate pool | Made the top 15 | Bad endings in top 15 (hand audit) | Late-season clips in top 15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rules only | 11/12 | 6/12 | 6 | 2 |
| Hybrid | 11/12 | 5/12 | 1 | 4 |

**What got better:**
- It dropped every "story at rest" ending the rules picked (ep 4+5 "Come.", ep 29+38 smile, ep 16, ep 18).
- It found the ep 3 rescue (00:56.20–01:24.00), the ep 2→3 stitch (rejected at the altar → a stranger floors a guard), the ep 24 strike (00:28.80–01:03.80), the ep 26 ring recognition and the ep 41 explosion.
- It independently proposed the ep 12 → ep 13 ultimatum→crawl stitch I built by hand in §2. Its version ends once the ring is found, which resolves the tension; mine ends mid-crawl.

**What got worse or didn't change:**
- **Score saturation:** nearly every proposal got hook 5 and cliffhanger 5, so the LLM scores don't separate good from great, and the blend lets the rules decide. The "rules vs LLM disagree" flag fired on 9 of 15 picks.
- **Selection lost the premise clips:** the ep 1 altar opener and the ep 3 proposal are in the pool but not the top 15. Batching (5 episodes per call) may cost per-episode attention: a single-episode call on ep 3 *did* return the proposal.
- **4 late-season clips** (ep 41–45) remain, even though the LLM's own strategy said to hold them. The rank pass's priority is only lightly weighted.
- **Paraphrased hooks:** the LLM wrote "The visceral image of…" where an editor needs the actual line. The report shows the script excerpt next to each one.
- ep 27's interrupted ring reveal is still not found by either engine.

**Conclusion:** finding moments is largely solved (11/12 in both pools); **choosing among them is the bottleneck**. The next steps are pairwise LLM comparisons instead of 1–5 scores, and showing the editor the top ~30 from the pool instead of a hard top 15.
